# AE46

A university Final Year Project (FYP) focused on Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS), with an initial emphasis on understanding and applying least squares to satellite positioning.

## Part 1: Least Squares and GNSS Positioning

The planned approach starts with the mathematics of least squares and builds towards its application in GNSS:

1. **Least squares fundamentals** — introduce the idea through fitting a straight line to measured data, showing how minimising the sum of squared residuals produces a best-fit solution.
2. **Pseudorange measurements** — explain how GNSS measurements relate to the distance between satellites and a receiver, including clock biases and measurement errors.
3. **Single Point Positioning (SPP)** — develop the pseudorange observation equations used to estimate receiver position and receiver clock bias.
4. **Least squares for positioning** — show how linearisation and iterative least squares can solve the nonlinear GNSS positioning equations.
